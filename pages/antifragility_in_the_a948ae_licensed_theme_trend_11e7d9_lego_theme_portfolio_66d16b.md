## Introduction

LEGO’s recent performance suggests that its resilience does not come from choosing between licensed and original themes. Instead, the company increasingly relies on a portfolio in which entertainment franchises absorb cultural attention while homegrown themes provide continuity, margins, brand identity and long-term stability. That balance matters in an antifragility context because it prevents LEGO from becoming dependent on the success of any single film studio, game publisher or entertainment cycle. When external franchises surge, LEGO can benefit. When those trends cool, internally owned themes such as City, Technic, Icons and Botanicals continue to generate demand and keep the building system at the centre of the business.

## Why licensed hits need proprietary anchors

Licensed themes are powerful, but they contain structural risks. LEGO does not control the underlying stories, release schedules, public reputation or long-term relevance of Star Wars, Harry Potter, Marvel or other franchises. Those assets belong to external rights holders.

A portfolio built entirely on licences would therefore expose LEGO to several forms of dependence:

* Royalty costs paid to intellectual-property owners.
* Contract renewals and [licensing]({{ 'licensing/' | relative_url }}) negotiations.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Shifts in public interest around specific franchises.
* Creative decisions made by film studios, publishers or media companies.
* Reputational issues affecting external brands.

</div>

Homegrown themes reduce that dependence. City can release police stations, trains, construction vehicles or fire stations regardless of cinema schedules. Technic can launch new engineering models without waiting for a movie premiere. Icons can draw from architecture, vehicles, nostalgia, design and display culture under LEGO’s own control.

This creates an important asymmetry. Licensed themes often require outside cultural energy to reach maximum impact, while proprietary themes can continue operating even when entertainment cycles weaken.

The company’s bestseller lists illustrate this stabilising effect. Across multiple reporting periods, LEGO’s strongest performers consistently include both licensed and internally owned themes rather than a rotating collection of entertainment properties. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lego.com/cdn/cs/sustainability/assets/bltc43f38d930d0440a/2025_Annual_Report_-_The_LEGO_Group.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lego.com">[LEGO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lego.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2025 Annual Report The LEGO Group</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Annual Report31 Dec 2025 — Investments for the future. In antifragility terms, licences function as optional growth engines, while proprietary themes act as the foundation that prevents success from becoming hostage to outside events.

## Botanicals and Icons show a different growth path

One of the most interesting recent developments is that some of LEGO’s fastest-growing themes are not traditional children's play lines and are not tied to entertainment franchises.

The Botanical Collection became prominent enough that LEGO repeatedly highlighted it in financial results, noting that it attracted new builders and performed strongly around gifting occasions. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lego.com/en-us/aboutus/news/2025/march/lego-group-delivers-record-results-in-2024" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lego.com">[LEGO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lego.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">lego group delivers record results in 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LEGO Group delivers record results in 2024 - About Us11 Mar 2025 — Bestselling themes combined homegrown and entertainment IP themes...</span></span></span>

This reveals a different source of demand. Customers buying a brick-built orchid or bouquet are often responding to interior decoration, gifting behaviour, mindfulness trends or lifestyle aesthetics rather than attachment to a film or television universe.

That mix creates several antifragile advantages.

**External successes become additive rather than essential.** If a Star Wars series, Harry Potter revival or gaming collaboration generates enthusiasm, LEGO can capture demand through licensed sets. But the business does not require every entertainment partner to succeed simultaneously.

**Internal themes accumulate value over decades.** LEGO owns City, Technic, Icons and Botanicals outright. Successful [investment]({{ 'investment/' | relative_url }}) in those brands strengthens assets the company fully controls rather than increasing dependence on licensors.

**Different themes respond to different consumer moods.** During periods when collectors are spending heavily, Icons may thrive. During gifting seasons, Botanicals may outperform. During major media releases, licensed ranges may surge. The company does not need one universal demand driver. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lego.com/en-us/aboutus/news/2025/august/the-lego-group-achieves-double-digit-top--and-bottom-line-growth-in-h1-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lego.com">[LEGO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lego.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the lego group achieves double digit top and bottom line growth in h1 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The LEGO Group H1 2025 - About Us27 Aug 2025 — Bestsellers included a mix of homegrown and licensed themes such as LEGO® City... **The [brick system]({{ 'brick-system/' | relative_url }}) remains the common platform.** Whether customers arrive through Star Wars, City, Fortnite, Technic or Botanicals, they enter the same ecosystem of compatible pieces, building habits and brand recognition. External stories bring attention, but the underlying LEGO experience remains internally controlled.

This is the crucial distinction. LEGO uses licences to absorb cultural volatility, but it avoids becoming a pure licensing company. Its recent bestseller lists show a portfolio where proprietary themes remain strong enough to anchor the business while licensed themes provide access to new audiences, trends and moments of attention.
